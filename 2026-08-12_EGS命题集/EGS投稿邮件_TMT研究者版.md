**Subject:** A testable extension of TMT: "guarantee fulfillment rate" as a missing variable

Dear Prof. Greenberg,

I am an independent researcher in China, and I have been deeply influenced by your work on terror management theory. After reading Pyszczynski, Solomon & Greenberg's 2015 chapter "Thirty Years of Terror Management Theory," I'd like to share a theoretical extension that I believe TMT has not yet addressed.

**The gap I see in TMT:** TMT explains how cultural worldviews buffer death anxiety, but it does not address what happens when the worldview's *promises fail to deliver*. When a society's existential guarantee system stops fulfilling its promises ("work hard → good life"), where does the death anxiety go? It doesn't disappear — it seeks new outlets.

**What I propose:** A construct I call "guarantee fulfillment rate" — the rate at which a society's existential guarantee system actually delivers on its promises. I have derived three testable hypotheses:

- **H1:** When perceived fulfillment rate declines, positive promises die first ("effort → reward" stops being believed), while negative punishments lag behind ("fear outlives promise"). This asymmetry predicts anomie, escape lines, declining fertility, and AI companionship.
- **H2:** Mortality salience effects are moderated by perceived fulfillment rate — individuals who have lost faith in the worldview's promises should show different terror management responses.
- **H3:** Cross-cultural: differences in fulfillment rate (China/Japan/Korea/US/Nordic) predict measurable differences in fertility, institutional trust, and non-conventional behavior.

**What I'm offering:** I have no empirical resources, but I can provide the full hypothesis set with operational definitions, ready for study design (attached). If the framework proves useful to your research program, I would be glad to participate in any way that is helpful, including contributing to the theoretical framing of resulting work — consistent with standard academic practice for theoretical contributions.

I have published a public series on this theory (in Chinese, on Zhihu, 2026). I'd be happy to share the English summary if you're interested.

Thank you for your work — it has been an intellectual foundation for me.

Sincerely,
