## Abstract

Terror Management Theory (TMT) explains how cultural worldviews buffer death anxiety, but it does not address what happens when the worldview's promises fail to deliver. This paper proposes a new construct — Guarantee Fulfillment Rate (GFR) — the rate at which a society's existential guarantee system actually delivers on its promises. Three core hypotheses are derived: (H1) when perceived GFR declines, positive promises die first while negative punishments lag behind, producing an asymmetry between fear and trust; (H2) mortality salience effects are moderated by perceived GFR — individuals who have lost faith in the worldview's promises show different terror management responses; (H3) cross-cultural differences in GFR predict measurable differences in fertility, institutional trust, and non-conventional behavior. A 2026-08-15 literature verification wave judged H1–H3 **partially supported** and promoted three further propositions: (H4) AI companions can function as a new existential guarantee system; (H5) guarantee failure bifurcates into escape vs. rebuilding (shift toward alternative guarantees); (H6) the theory's "two kinds of normal" can be operationalized via social-intolerance time series (WVS/ISSP, free public data). Operational definitions, study designs, and public data sources are provided for each hypothesis. Falsification conditions are explicitly stated and separated from branch-decision conditions. The framework is offered freely to the research community under CC BY 4.0.

## 1. Theoretical Gap

**TMT's contribution:** Terror Management Theory has established that death anxiety is buffered by cultural worldviews; mortality salience enhances worldview defense, self-esteem striving, and ingroup bias (Greenberg, Pyszczynski & Solomon, 1986, book chapter; Rosenblatt et al., 1989; review: Pyszczynski, Solomon & Greenberg, 2015).

**The gap:** TMT explains how worldviews buffer death anxiety, but not **where death anxiety goes when the worldview's promises stop being fulfilled**. When a society's existential guarantee system ("work hard → good life") visibly fails, individuals do not simply stop fearing death — the fear remains while trust in the guarantees declines.

**EGS theory's claim:** Every society contains an existential guarantee system (EGS) composed of positive promises ("if you do X, you get Y") and negative sanctions ("if you don't do X, you suffer Z"). The theory's core, empirically testable claim is that **these two components decay asymmetrically**: positive promise expectations collapse faster than negative sanction expectations. "Fear outlives promise." *(First axiom — preserved word-for-word from v1.1; no verification result has modified, weakened, or deleted it.)*

---

## 2. Core Construct: Guarantee Fulfillment (three levels, not one)

The v1 document conflated three distinct constructs. They are separated here:

```
Objective Fulfillment   — How much society ACTUALLY delivered in the past
                              (macro indicators: intergenerational mobility,
                               real wage growth vs GDP, education returns)

Perceived Fulfillment   — How much the INDIVIDUAL believes society delivered
                              (retrospective perception)

Expected Fulfillment    — How much the individual EXPECTS society will deliver
                              (prospective expectation)  ← THEORETICAL CORE
```

**Why Expected GFR is the theoretical core:** EGS theory is not about whether society *did* deliver; it is about whether individuals still *trust* the system to deliver *in the future*. Behavior (marriage, fertility, effort, escape) is driven by forward-looking expectations, not retrospective accounting. This is the construct that should anchor H1 and H2.

**Verification note (2026-08-15):** The three-level split is supported by the verification wave — the side-report literature anchors map onto the Perceived/Expected side (e.g., perceived social mobility and opportunity beliefs ≈ Perceived/Expected GFR's positive-promise side, Day & Fiske 2017 [H1-3.1]; McCall et al. 2017 [H1-3.2]). Expected GFR itself still has **zero direct measurement** in the literature (see §9, limitation 1).

## 3. Core Mechanism: Guarantee Asymmetry

```
Expected GFR (low)
      ↓
Positive Promise Expectation ↓↓  (collapses fast)
Negative Sanction Expectation ↓   (declines slowly)
      ↓
Guarantee Asymmetry ↑
      (gap between what you fear and what you believe you'll gain)
```

**Guarantee Asymmetry Index (GAI)** — operationalized as a standardized difference:

```
GAI = Z(Negative Sanction Expectation) − Z(Positive Promise Expectation)
```

Higher GAI = the system's punishments weigh heavier than its promises (fear dominates hope). This replaces the v1 "fear/trust ratio," which had undesirable distributional properties. The verification wave supports the difference-score choice over the ratio (the ratio has poor distributional properties; the P1.2 path test maps directly onto the GAI mechanism).

## 4. H1 (Core, Single Mechanism)

> **H1: Lower expected guarantee fulfillment is associated with higher Guarantee Asymmetry — negative sanction expectations decline less steeply than positive promise expectations.**

Formal statement:

> **H1: Declining expected guarantee fulfillment will be associated with a greater asymmetry between negative sanction expectations and positive reward expectations, such that negative sanction expectations decline less steeply than positive reward expectations.** *(First axiom in testable form — unchanged.)*

**Domain restriction (critical):** The first study tests ONE domain — **education/occupational returns** ("study hard → good school → good job → good income"). Marriage and homeownership sanctions are excluded from Study 1 because they involve distinct mechanisms (social vs. identity/status sanctions). One mechanism at a time.

**Boundary condition (added 2026-08-15):** The slow decay of negative sanction expectations is expected only for **high-probability, foreseeable punishments** (e.g., employment and income exclusion in the education/occupational domain); **low-probability, vague punishments** (e.g., criminal punishment) may collapse along with promise trust (Nagin & Telep 2017 [H1-3.6]). v1.1's single-domain restriction naturally falls inside this boundary — consistency statement *(synthesis-level inference: the H1 side report recommended high-probability sanction classes such as employment/marriage-social-exclusion; the education/occupational narrowing itself is a synthesis-layer connection argument, audit P2-11)*. *Audit note: this boundary explicitates the first axiom's applicability domain; it does not modify the axiom itself.*

**Operational definitions (education/occupational domain):**

| Variable | Definition | Measurement |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| Expected GFR (education domain) | "How likely is it that studying hard will lead to a good job in your future?" | 5-7 point Likert, 4-6 items (prospective framing) |
| Positive promise expectation | Expected reward from participating in the education/occupational system | "If I invest in education, I will get: income security / social status / career autonomy" (Likert) |
| Negative sanction expectation | Expected cost of NOT participating — **framed as perceived probability** ("How LIKELY am I to lose: income security / social status / respect if I drop out of this competitive system?") | Likert, probability framing¹ |
| GAI | Asymmetry between the two | Z(sanction) − Z(promise), computed per individual |

> ¹ *Per the deterrence literature, the behavioral constraint is driven primarily by the perceived **certainty (probability)** of punishment, not its severity; the severity dimension is optional and rests on weaker evidence (Nagin 2013 [H1-3.4]). This probability-framing revision applies to the whole negative-sanction side of EGS scales.*

**Testable predictions:**
- **P1.1:** Expected GFR is negatively correlated with GAI (r expected ≈ .3–.5)
  - **Verification status (2026-08-15): supported (positive-side link).** The causal link "low Expected GFR → low positive promise expectation" has 2 independent experimental lines + 1 medical cohort: Day & Fiske 2017 (low-social-mobility framing reduced meritocratic/just-world beliefs, across the political spectrum) [H1-3.1]; McCall et al. 2017 (exposure to rising inequality increased skepticism about opportunity) [H1-3.2]; Kuper et al. 2002 (effort–reward imbalance measurable with physiological consequences, Whitehall II cohort) [H1-3.3]. **Caveat:** v1.1's P1.1 is a GFR–GAI correlation; the literature supports the "positive side declines" component, while the full GAI relationship still awaits EGS's own data.
- **P1.2:** The GFR→GAI relationship is driven by positive promise expectations declining faster than negative sanction expectations (tested via paired comparisons / structural path)
  - **Verification status (2026-08-15): partially supported (indirect); literature gap — EGS's most original prediction.** Fear-side survival has an independent deterrence literature (Nagin 2013 [H1-3.4]); behavioral maintenance after promise collapse has indirect hints (Whitehall II cohort kept working under effort–reward imbalance [H1-3.3]); anomie-scale work supports construct validity (Teymoori et al. 2017 [H1-3.5]). **No study has ever compared trust-vs.-fear decay timing within one design** — P1.2 is an EGS-original prediction with no direct precedent (the largest gap; see §9, limitation 2). **Behavioral-layer gap (explicit, audit P2-5):** no retrieved study directly links the "promise-trust collapsed but punishment-fear persists" state to specific escape behaviors (non-marriage, fertility refusal, exit from effort) — H3's macro evidence covers this only indirectly.
- **P1.3:** P1.1–P1.2 hold after controlling for generalized pessimism, institutional trust, relative deprivation, perceived social mobility, and future orientation (see Section 7)
  - **Verification status (2026-08-15): partially supported.** Day & Fiske 2017 reports robustness across the political spectrum [H1-3.1]; Kuper et al. 2002 is a cohort study with occupational-grade controls [H1-3.3]; the full control battery requires EGS's own data.
- **P1.4 (extension, exploratory; merged from H8 "fear's dark side" — added 2026-08-15):** Individuals with high perceived punitive pressure show **desire shutdown** — significantly lower aspiration levels and new-goal pursuit motivation ("dare not want," alongside "dare not stop"). This is the second effect channel of the punitive system (motivational suppression), measured in the **same sample as H1** to reduce cost. Mechanism basis: learned helplessness under uncontrollable punishment (Maier & Seligman 2016 [挖-证据#17]); adjacent construct: cruel optimism — persistent attachment to already-damaged promise objects (Berlant 2006/2011 [挖-证据#18]). Construct boundary (vs. depression/burnout) must be clarified during scale development.
- **P1.5 (extension, exploratory; P8.2 of the merged H8 preserved — added 2026-08-15):** Individuals with high perceived punitive pressure hold significantly **higher expectations of post-goal disappointment** ("the reference point is pushed to the ceiling" mechanism — even achieved goals disappoint). A longitudinal variant (P8.3 of H8) predicts that desire-shutdown indicators rise with exposure duration in high-punitive-pressure environments (e.g., harsh appraisal-driven workplaces); retained here as an exploratory longitudinal extension of H1.

## 5. H2 (Moderation, Directional — revised 2026-08-15)

> **H2: Expected GFR moderates the relationship between mortality salience and worldview defense.**

**Rationale:** TMT predicts mortality salience → worldview defense. EGS predicts this classic effect is conditional: when individuals no longer expect the system to deliver, the defense of the *existing* worldview reorganizes.

**Directional prediction (revised 2026-08-15; replaces the v1.1 "weaker" prediction):**

> **H2: Among individuals with low (vs. high) Expected GFR, the mortality salience response takes the form of a defense-structure shift: affirmative defense of the existing system (system justification / endorsement) weakens; punitive defense (outgroup derogation / harsh judgment) does not decrease — it may even increase — but its target can migrate; openness to alternative systems is measured as an exploratory outcome with no hypothesized direction.**

**Why the v1.1 "weaker" direction was revised (conflict made explicit):** The nearest-neighbor evidence points the opposite way: insecure attachment (a low-relationship-guarantee proxy) produced **stronger** harsh judgments after mortality salience (Mikulincer & Florian 2000 [H2-3]); classic TMT DVs (judge-bail / outgroup derogation) are precisely punitive-defense measures, so predicting "weaker" on them was the riskiest choice. The revised prediction is consistent with the evidence and internally consistent with H1's fear-residue claim (§6f).

**Dual-channel declaration (added 2026-08-15, audit P1-3):** H1's "fear outlives promise" and H2's "affirmative defense weakens / punitive defense persists-or-migrates" are coordinated by layering fear into two channels — a **behavioral-constraint channel** (punishment fear → not daring to exit; supported by deterrence evidence [H1-3.4][H1-3.6]) and a **meaning-supply channel** (death-anxiety buffering → can migrate; supported by attachment/religion evidence [H2-3][H2-4][H2-5]). Each channel has its own independent evidence line, but **no study has ever measured both channels in one design; dual-channel independence is a theoretical hypothesis, not an established finding.** It must be tested jointly (H1 P1.2 timing + H2 defense-structure shift; see §8, branch-decision conditions). *(Illustrative extrapolation, not evidence: the "present in body, absent in mind" state matches the Korea case pattern only as a synthesis-level inference — the Korea side report contains TFR and subsidy data only, no behavioral data on "still working while not marrying/having children.")*

**Operational design:**
- 2 (mortality salience vs. control) × measured Expected GFR (median split or continuous moderator)
- DV — three categories (per the H2 side report recommendation): (a) **punitive defense** (outgroup derogation; judge bail paradigm — Rosenblatt et al., 1989); (b) **affirmative defense** (system justification / endorsement of the existing system); (c) **alternative-system openness** (exploratory; measured, **no hypothesized direction** — consistent with v1.1's "not hypothesized direction")
- **Mandatory co-measures:** attachment (ECR), religious commitment, and Personal Need for Structure (PNS) as control moderators — otherwise the interaction cannot be attributed to GFR uniquely
- N ≥ 200 per condition (based on typical TMT effect sizes and the need to detect moderation)

**What H2 does NOT claim (v1 overreach removed):**
- Does NOT make a directional prediction of "redirected" defense toward alternative worldviews / new religions / radical ideologies (alternative-system openness is exploratory measurement, not a directional hypothesis)
- Does NOT claim to explain Many Labs 4 heterogeneity

**Many Labs 4 framing (maintained 2026-08-15, no change):** Many Labs 4 (17 labs, N = 1,550, g = 0.07, 95% CI [−0.03, 0.17]; heterogeneity not significant) demonstrates that the classic mortality salience effect may not be robust under the tested conditions. EGS offers GFR as a **prospective moderator hypothesis to be tested independently** — not as an explanation of that null result. This framing is retained because the replication failures are real (ML4 [H2-1]; Sætrevik & Sjåstad 2022 [H2-6]) but carry a structural sample tension: ML4 failed precisely in the US-student population where the meta-analysis shows the strongest effect (Burke et al. 2010 [H2-2]) — explaining ML4 via GFR would require the extra, un-evidenced claim that 2010s students have much lower GFR than 1990s samples.

## 6. H3 (Deferred — Future Research, revised 2026-08-15)

The v1 cross-cultural macro hypothesis (national GFR → fertility / institutional trust / escape behavior) is **demoted from core to future research**. Rationale (verified 2026-08-15 — the verification wave confirmed that no composite GFR index has ever been constructed [H3-缺口1]):

1. It is a research program, not a hypothesis (8 outcome variables, each with extensive alternative explanations)
2. A national "GFR composite index" risks construct contamination (overlapping with GDP, housing, income, education — i.e., an over-composite index)
3. It cannot be tested credibly before the individual-level construct is validated

**Future sequence (after H1/H2; verification priors injected 2026-08-15):**
- **Study 3: country-level institutional fulfillment → institutional trust.** Verification prior: the strongest component-level prior is **individual-level** — the Chinese hukou reform quasi-experiment shows upward mobility → significantly higher trust in government (Huang 2020 [H3-证据3]). *Level-jump caveat (audit P2-6): Huang 2020 is an individual-level hukou-conversion → government-trust result; no published cross-national "composite GFR → composite trust index" test exists, so the country-level replication remains open.*
- **Study 4: country-level GFR → fertility / demographic behavior** (with proper controls and measurement invariance work). Component evidence: trust–fertility (Aassve et al. 2020, Italian province panel during the Great Recession [H3-证据2]); housing-affordability–fertility (Kostelecký & Vobecká 2009, Czech time series [H3-证据4]); Korea 28-year case (TFR 1996 1.57 → 2024 0.75, global lowest, despite ~280 trillion won of pronatalist spending [H3-证据5]). **East Asian specificity controls (audit P2-3):** Korea/Japan/Taiwan ("high spending, low TFR") and the Nordics ("high spending, relatively higher TFR") coexist — GFR is not the only explanation; cultural/gender-norm interactions must be declared as control variables and interaction terms.

**Prediction revisions (2026-08-15):**
- **P3.1 threshold revised:** r ≥ 0.5 → "**direction + significance + effect size reporting (expected 0.3–0.5)**." The literature's typical structural-variable effects on TFR are far below 0.5 (Gauthier & Gietel-Basten 2024 [H3-证据6]); an r ≥ 0.5 threshold would fail unfalsifiably. Component directions are supported (trust–fertility [H3-证据2]; housing–fertility [H3-证据4]; Korea [H3-证据5]).
- **P3.2 (GFR ↔ institutional trust): partially supported (strongest this round)** — Huang 2020 [H3-证据3], S-level direct correspondence.
- **P3.3 (GFR ↔ escape, negative): partially supported** — "deaths of despair" (Case & Deaton 2015 [H3-证据1]) directly corresponds to "promise failure → escapist pathology" with national specificity; Korea's non-marriage/childlessness is consistent. New-type escape indicators (AI companion use) have no macro time series (carried by H4 at the individual level).
- **P3.4 (lag 1–2 five-year periods): evidence insufficient (one strong case).** Korea's 28-year series fits the form [H3-证据5]; **as of 2026-08-15 no published study has tested the 1–2 five-year-lag specification — this prediction is EGS-unique and untested.** *Specification note added (R6).*
- **Causality declaration (added):** Daruich & Kozlowski 2020 [H3-补] documents a fertility → intergenerational-mobility channel, opposite in direction to H3's mobility → fertility claim. Designs must use panel fixed effects / IV, or reframe as "bidirectional reinforcement."

## 6b. Future Research: Rebuilding Line (H5 — formal proposition, added 2026-08-15)

> **H5: When EGS fulfillment declines (guarantee failure), individual reactions bifurcate: escape (exiting the guarantee structure: lying flat / withdrawal / refusal) vs. rebuilding (shifting toward or attaching to an alternative guarantee: religion, subculture, AI companion, ideology). The availability and quality of alternative guarantees determine the bifurcation direction.**

- **P5.1 (individual/quasi-experimental):** After an exogenous guarantee shock (disaster / crisis / unemployment), religious participation and meaning-system investment rise significantly (shift toward an alternative EGS).
  - **Verification status: supported.** One quasi-experiment — Sibley & Bulbulia 2012, Christchurch earthquake: religious faith increased among the earthquake-affected in a longitudinal probability sample [挖-证据#6] — plus one macro time series — Bentzen 2021, prayer rose during COVID-19 (search-data proxy) [挖-证据#7]. *(Precise labeling per audit P2-9: a quasi-experiment + a macro time series, NOT "two natural experiments.")*
- **P5.2 (mechanism):** After meaning threat, individuals reaffirm alternative meanings in different domains (fluid compensation) — the psychological name of the rebuilding line.
  - **Verification status: mechanism supported.** The Meaning Maintenance Model (Heine et al. 2006 [挖-证据#8]) documents fluid compensation: threat in domain A, reaffirmation in domain B. EGS's rebuilding line = MMM's existential-level version.
- **P5.3 (macro):** During old-EGS failure periods, new guarantee supply (religious revival / new movements / AI companion penetration) and destructive escape (addiction / suicide / "deaths of despair") show a substitution relationship.
  - **Verification status: literature gap — EGS-original prediction.** No published study tests alternative-guarantee availability as the moderator of escape direction. Connectable to H3's deaths-of-despair evidence (Case & Deaton 2015 [H3-证据1]). Reverse-causality caveat (people high in religiosity may escape less for other reasons) must be handled.

**P1-2 framing (audit correction):** The evidence for P5.1 supports **rebuilding/shift toward existing alternative guarantees** (religion is an existing system being re-engaged) — NOT the formation of an entirely new guarantee system ("new coding"). **New coding formation (H4 P4.3) remains un-evidenced** and must not be presented as established.

## 6c. Future Research: AI Companion as New EGS (H4 — formal proposition, added 2026-08-15)

> **H4: Individuals who form sustained relationships with AI companions (Replika / Character.AI / similar) treat the companion as an existential guarantee source (unconditional acceptance + companionship + meaning supply).**

- **P4.1:** AI attachment strength is positively correlated with loneliness / lack of real-world social support (AI fills the missing guarantee).
  - **Verification status: supported (S-level direct).** Maples et al. 2024 — survey of 1,006 Replika student users: lonelier than typical student populations yet perceiving high social support; 3% reported Replika halted their suicidal ideation [挖-证据#5]. *Boundaries: cross-sectional, self-selected sample — cannot establish causality; supports "AI provides support," NOT "defense is taken over under mortality salience" (P4.2 remains un-evidenced).* Construct foundation: attachment theory as a framework for human–AI relationships (Xie & Pentina 2022 [挖-证据#2]); relationship development from curiosity to trust and self-disclosure (Skjuve et al. 2021 [挖-证据#3]); parasocial relationships' existential function (Semmler 2016, B-level [挖-证据#4]).
- **P4.2:** AI attachment strength is negatively correlated with death/existential anxiety; after a mortality salience prime, high-AI-attachment individuals show significantly weaker worldview defense (defense is "taken over" by the alternative guarantee, rather than intensified).
  - **Verification status: literature gap — the largest original contribution of H4.** No direct experimental test of AI companion × death anxiety / mortality salience exists. (Recommended search terms from the side report: "AI companion death anxiety" / "parasocial relationship terror management experimental" / "chatbot mortality salience worldview defense" / "attachment to AI mortality salience".)
- **P4.3 (adjudicative):** If P4.1 + P4.2 both hold → the AI companion forms a relational institution independent of its commodity form → **"new EGS formation" (formula-chain New Coding) is established**. If only P4.1 holds and P4.2 fails → the AI companion is a **"co-opted commodity providing parasocial comfort"** (Co-optation).
  - **Position:** This adjudicates the author's project positioning (co-opted commodity vs. new-EGS experiment) and the theory's unresolved question of the co-optation / EGS-ization boundary; the dispute card window is 2025–2027. This is a **branch-decision design, not a theory-falsification condition** (see §8).

**Suggested designs:** (1) cross-sectional scales (ECR adapted to AI + death anxiety + meaning + loneliness; N ≥ 300; heavy AI-companion users vs. controls); (2) 2 × 2 experiment (mortality salience × AI attachment; DVs = worldview defense + alternative-system openness); (3) longitudinal panel of AI attachment formation vs. death anxiety and real-relationship investment.

## 6d. Future Research: Two Kinds of "Normal" (H6 — formal operationalization, added 2026-08-15)

> **H6: Normative "normal" (the living code) can be operationalized by the strength of social rejection/intolerance toward NOT doing X. This fills H3's "GFR has no index" gap with a cheaper, free, public-data macro channel.**

- **P6.1:** Social intolerance (stigma/rejection attitudes) toward a behavior (non-marriage / cohabitation / childfree / no housing purchase) is significantly negatively correlated with that behavior's prevalence.
  - Construct-isomorphism evidence: injunctive vs. descriptive norms (Schultz et al. 2007 [挖-证据#10]); singlehood as identity formation (Kislev 2023 [挖-证据#11]); second demographic transition, 1986–2020 update (Lesthaeghe 2020 [挖-证据#12]).
- **P6.2 (judgment-method test):** Repulsion-force decline (de-stigmatization) **precedes or accompanies** the rise in behavior prevalence — the theory card's "repulsion declining → code failing" judgment is converted into a time-series test.
  - **Verification status: literature gap — no published study tests "social intolerance change leads behavior change."** (Recommended search terms: "stigma decline behavior change longitudinal" / "injunctive norm change precede behavior" / "de-stigmatization singlehood fertility".)
- **P6.3:** Intergenerational conflict quantified: the previous generation's repulsion-decline curve × the next generation's behavior-spread curve cross (the observable form of "experiential normal" vs. "normative normal" conflict).

**Data sources (free/public):** WVS/ISSP attitude time series (1990–2025) — free and public, and avoids the composite-index construct contamination that sank the H3 macro channel. **Suggested designs:** acceptability-item time series × country behavior rates; de-stigmatization event (policy/media shift) breakpoint analysis; cross-cultural comparison (China/Japan/Korea/Nordic repulsion differences). *Method note: attitude (scale) and behavior (repulsion practices) must be distinguished to avoid common-source bias.*

## 6e. Exploratory Leads (not formal hypotheses — added 2026-08-15)

Retained from the new-problem-mining wave with explicit status. These are leads, not formal propositions; they lack settled quantitative test methods or their constructs overlap existing ones.

- **H7 Co-optation iron law ("the harder to commodify, the harder to co-opt"):** P7.1 within one escape form, consumable forms are co-opted first while the refusal behavior itself resists; P7.2 across escape forms, commodifiable escapes (cool culture / rebellious style) are co-opted faster than non-commodifiable ones (core refusal behaviors of lying flat); P7.3 mechanism = brands/industries convert escape symbols into cultural capital. Mechanism theory is mature (Holt 2002 [挖-证据#14]; Spracklen & Spracklen 2013 [挖-证据#15]; Hebdige 1979 [挖-证据#16]); **quantitative tests are absent** — keep as a qualitative auxiliary line to H4.
- **H9 Cognitive EGS:** P9.1 need for closure ↔ dogmatism/ideological extremity (heavily overlaps the existing need-for-closure construct); P9.2 cognitive threat → frozen judgment; **P9.3 (EGS-unique, retained as an exploratory cross-layer prediction):** low perceived GFR ↔ high need for closure — the cross-layer coupling of the two EGS layers (Webster & Kruglanski 1997 [挖-证据#19]).
- **H10 Corporate testbed (phase-2 project):** P10.1 corporate mortality is age-dependent per organizational ecology (Freeman et al. 1983 [挖-证据#21]; Hannan 1998 [挖-证据#22]); P10.2/P10.3 "continuity migration / death-definition rewriting" constructs are blank in the literature; high data cost and hard causal identification → schedule after H4–H6.

## 6f. The "Guarantee Shift" Proposition Family (unifying statement — added 2026-08-15)

H2 (revised: defense-object migration) + H4 (AI companion takes over death defense) + H5 (rebuilding line / fluid compensation) are **three levels of the same mechanism** — "death anxiety / meaning threat does not disappear; it only changes its guarantee object":

| Level | Proposition | Key evidence | Status |
|-------|------------|--------------|--------|
| Psychological | H2 (revised): defense-object migration | Attachment: the same death prime → completely different reactions in different people [H2-3]; unified by MMM [挖-证据#8] | Mechanism supported; GFR construct untested |
| Technological | H4: AI companion takes over | P4.1 supported (Maples 2024 [挖-证据#5]); P4.2 gap | Partially supported |
| Macro | H5: religious / new-movement revival | P5.1: two independent channels [挖-证据#6][挖-证据#7] | **Supported (strongest link)** |

**Boundary (conditions for the family to hold):** The shift is constrained by **alternative-guarantee availability** — it occurs mainly among people with partial alternative commitments (Norenzayan & Hansen 2006: supernatural-belief shift occurred mainly among the religiously affiliated [H2-5]); individuals with no alternative commitment at all may show "escape / meaning suspension" rather than shift (H5 P5.3's macro substitution remains an EGS-original gap prediction). H4's adjudicative role: if the AI companion is a "harder-to-commodify" alternative guarantee, it offers a new shift target for the pure no-commitment population — **H4 is the family's complement experiment on the extreme-low-GFR group (the literature's blind spot).**

**Coordination with H1 (conflict made explicit and resolved):** H1 says "fear outlives promise" (low GFR → fear residue); v1.0–v1.1's H2 said "low GFR → weaker defense." If fear persists and fear drives defense, defense cannot simply weaken — the surface contradiction is resolved by layering fear (see §5 dual-channel declaration). After R4, H1 and H2 are parallel-isomorphic: trust/affirmative defense = positive-promise side; fear/punitive defense = negative-sanction side. **Per audit P1-3, this coordination is a theoretical verdict statement to be tested jointly (H1 P1.2 timing + H2 defense-structure shift); dual-channel independence is a hypothesis, not an established finding.**

## 7. Alternative Explanations (must be ruled out in Study 1)

The unique value of EGS depends on GFR predicting GAI **beyond** established constructs. The first study must control for:

| Alternative | Relation to GFR/GAI | Control strategy |
|-------------|-------------------|------------------|
| Generalized pessimism | Might drive both low expectations and high fear | Control scale (e.g., Life Orientation Test — LOT-R, dispositional optimism) |
| Institutional trust | Could be a proxy for the same construct | Control scale (trust in government/market/science) |
| Relative deprivation | Perceived unfairness could produce asymmetry | Control scale (relative deprivation measure) |
| Perceived social mobility | Overlaps with GFR conceptually | Control (MacArthur ladder / mobility beliefs) |
| Future orientation | Time perspective affects expectations | Control (Consideration of Future Consequences scale) |

**Key falsification criterion:** If GFR adds no incremental predictive validity beyond these controls, EGS's distinctive construct value is challenged. This is the honest test.

## 8. Falsification Conditions (updated 2026-08-15)

### 8.1 Theory-falsification conditions

**EGS is wrong if:**
1. P1.1 fails (GFR uncorrelated with GAI) → the asymmetry mechanism is wrong
2. P1.3 fails (GFR adds nothing beyond generalized pessimism / institutional trust) → GFR is not a distinctive construct
3. H2 fails (mortality salience × GFR interaction not significant, adequately powered) → the TMT moderation claim is wrong
4. Replication failure of P1.1–P1.2 in a preregistered second sample → the asymmetry is not robust
5. **(new) P6.2 fails** (repulsion-force decline does not lead behavior change) → the "two kinds of normal" judgment method is wrong

**Note:** A null H2 is informative but not fatal to EGS — H1 is the core claim. H2 is the bridge to TMT literature.

### 8.2 Branch-decision conditions (NOT theory falsification — added 2026-08-15, audit P2-10)

These designs adjudicate between branches of the formula chain (guarantee failure → escape line → [co-optation | new coding]); either outcome is an informative decision, not a theory failure:

- **P4.2 fails** (high AI attachment individuals defend MORE, not less, after mortality salience) → the AI companion is an **anesthetic-type commodity** rather than a new EGS — this decides the co-optation vs. new-coding branch for AI companions.
- **P4.3 decision rule:** P4.1 + P4.2 both hold → new EGS formation; only P4.1 → co-opted commodity.
- **Dual-channel joint test:** H1 P1.2 (timing) + H2 (defense-structure shift) must measure both fear channels in one design; the dual-channel coordination of §5/§6f is confirmed only if the joint test supports it.

### 8.3 Theoretical boundaries (updated 2026-08-15)

- **First-axiom applicability domain:** the "sanctions lag behind promises" claim applies to high-probability, foreseeable punishments (education/occupational employment and income exclusion); it is not asserted for low-probability, vague punishments (R3; §4 boundary condition).
- **H3 composite index:** demoted; P3.1 threshold downgraded from r ≥ 0.5 to direction + significance + effect size (expected 0.3–0.5) (§6).
- **H2 shift direction:** bounded by alternative-guarantee availability; no directional prediction for alternative-system openness (exploratory only) (§5).
- **Dual-channel independence:** a hypothesis for joint testing, not an established finding (P1-3; §5, §6f).
- **New coding formation (H4 P4.3):** remains un-evidenced; H5's evidence supports rebuilding/shift toward existing alternatives only (P1-2; §6b, §6c).

## 9. Verification Limitations (2026-08-15 — added)

Honest boundaries of the verification wave that produced the revision notes above:

1. **Expected GFR construct: zero direct testing.** No retrieved study measures "perceived fulfillment rate of society's promises" or its equivalent and tests it as a moderator/mediator. All H1–H6 propositions presuppose the scale validation (Study 1a) of the Measurement Development Plan (§10; v1.1 §9). Until the scale exists, H2–H6 rest on "literature-grounded original hypotheses," not verified propositions.
2. **Literature-gap predictions (EGS-original, no direct precedent):** P1.2 (trust/fear decay timing), P4.2 (AI attachment × mortality salience), P5.3 (alternative-guarantee supply vs. destructive escape), P6.2 (intolerance change leading behavior change), P3.4 (1–2 five-year lag specification). These are the components "most in need of own experiments."
3. **East Asia: direct EGS-designed tests missing.** The verification evidence is almost entirely North American/European (Whitehall II UK; US samples; Italy; Czech; New Zealand). Huang 2020 (China) and the Korea case are **existing studies, not EGS-designed tests** — the proposition set's suggested China/US/Japan-Korea sample designs have not been covered by any EGS-designed study.
4. **Abstract-level judgment.** All side-report judgments are based on abstract-layer + bibliographic metadata (no full-text reading); cited figures (e.g., Maples et al. 2024's 3%) appear only where the API abstract text was verified.
5. **r ≥ 0.5 threshold downgraded (H3).** The evidence suggests typical structural-variable effects on TFR fall in the 0.3–0.5 range; the v1.0 threshold was too strong to be falsifiable (Gauthier & Gietel-Basten 2024 [H3-证据6]).
6. **Dual-channel independence untested (P1-3).** The behavioral-constraint vs. meaning-supply layering of fear has independent evidence lines per channel but has never been jointly tested in one design; it is a hypothesis (§5, §6f).
7. **H2 side report lists three unverified leads not counted as evidence** (Sterling et al. 2016; Juhl & Routledge 2010; Landau et al. 2004) — they are leads for future work, not support.
8. **Sample-tension honesty (H2 P2.3):** explaining Many Labs 4 via GFR would require the extra, un-evidenced claim that 2010s Western student samples have much lower GFR than 1990s samples; the v1.1 "motivating puzzle + candidate moderator" framing is retained instead.

## 10. Measurement Development Plan (updated 2026-08-15)

Before hypothesis testing, the Expected GFR scale must be validated (this is Study 1a):

1. **Item generation:** 10-12 items across 3 domains (education/occupation, marriage/family, housing/status) — but analyze the education/occupation domain first
2. **Content validity:** expert review (2-3 social/personality psychologists)
3. **Pilot:** N ≈ 150, EFA to check factor structure (expected: single "Expected Fulfillment" factor per domain, or a higher-order general factor)
4. **Validation:** N ≈ 300, CFA, test-retest (2-4 weeks), convergent/discriminant validity against institutional trust and optimism
5. **Measurement invariance:** test across age/gender/education groups before cross-cultural use

**Item-source links to existing paradigms (added 2026-08-15, R13):**
- Positive-promise-expectation items can be adapted from social-mobility-belief / meritocratic-belief paradigms (Day & Fiske 2017 [H1-3.1]).
- The Perception of Anomie Scale — PAS (Teymoori et al. 2017 [H1-3.5]) and effort–reward imbalance — ERI (Siegrist model; Kuper et al. 2002 [H1-3.3]) serve as convergent-validity references: GFR has literature anchors, the scale need not start from zero.
- P1.4/P1.5 (desire shutdown, post-goal disappointment) items require new development with explicit construct-boundary work vs. depression/burnout (Maier & Seligman 2016 [挖-证据#17] as mechanism basis).

## 11. Proposed Study 1 (Core Study; updated 2026-08-15)

**Design:** Cross-sectional survey, preregistered

- Sample: N ≥ 300 adults, single country (China first — where the phenomenon is most visible)
- Measures: Expected GFR (new scale), Positive promise expectation, Negative sanction expectation (probability framing; → GAI), controls (pessimism, trust, relative deprivation, mobility beliefs, future orientation), demographics; **plus P1.4/P1.5 desire-shutdown and post-goal-disappointment items in the same sample (shared-sample cost reduction)**
- Analysis: correlational + hierarchical regression (GFR after controls); path model for P1.2
- **Preregistration:** OSF preregistration before data collection (this is non-negotiable for credibility)

**Success criterion:** GFR predicts GAI with incremental validity beyond all controls (ΔR² significant, effect size reported with CI).

## 12. Proposed Study 2 (Experimental; updated 2026-08-15)

**Design:** 2 (mortality salience vs. dental-pain control) × continuous Expected GFR

- Same sample frame as Study 1 or a fresh sample (N ≥ 200/cell)
- DV — three categories (revised per R4): (a) punitive defense (classic paradigm); (b) affirmative defense (system justification); (c) alternative-system openness (exploratory, **no hypothesized direction**)
- **Mandatory co-measures:** attachment (ECR), religious commitment, PNS — control moderators, otherwise the interaction cannot be attributed to GFR uniquely
- Prediction: defense-structure shift — at low Expected GFR, affirmative defense of the existing system weakens while punitive defense does not decrease (target may migrate); alternative-system openness measured, not directional
- Secondary DV (exploratory): alternative worldview openness (measured, not hypothesized direction)

- Country-level analysis with careful construct development (dimensions, weights, measurement invariance, sensitivity analysis)
- Single pathway at a time: institutional fulfillment → institutional trust (individual-level quasi-causal prior: Huang 2020 [H3-证据3]; country-level replication open — level-jump caveat); then GFR → fertility (component evidence: trust–fertility [H3-证据2]; housing–fertility [H3-证据4]; Korea [H3-证据5])
